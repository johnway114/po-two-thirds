# Internal adversarial audit — unrefereed

**Outcome:** no mathematical blocker found for the stated family, neighborhood, or strict one-surplus result. This is an internal AI-assisted audit, not peer review or a formal proof in a theorem prover. No outside economist has confirmed the results.

- **Center, independent mathematical check.** One reader checked the original matrix against v2, derived the four maximum trimmed values, and gave the column-loss proof of integral PO without consulting `check.py`. The smallest nonzero loss is one even for real K. Allowing *fractional* transfers instead would invalidate the PO witness; the theorem does not do that. The paper states its upper bound for integer K, so we separately check the real interval.
- **Symbolic certificate, second implementation.** A separate reviewer reconstructed the affine sign test using vertices of the closed intervals and directions of unbounded growth. They checked open versus closed faces, the common-K relaxation, all comparison goods, and the partition enumeration. They independently recovered the 255 off-center cases before the list of witnesses was shortened. They also reported brute-force checks at three rational points per off-center case, and exact center optima at K=6, 7, 8, 13/2 and 61/10. Those samples are cross-checks, not the continuous proof. The final [receipt](RECEIPT.json) checks five allocations covering all 255 cases directly.
- **Upper bound, independent code review.** The reviewer checked the coefficient expansion in K−6, the requirement of a nonempty trimmed bundle for exclusion, and the requirement that the *same* allocation weakly improve all four rows and strictly improve one. A coefficient test that fails is inconclusive; the checker therefore requires a separate Pareto improvement for every leftover assignment. The integral PO witness at equality is not rejected by an impossibility claim about equality.
- **Arbitrary perturbations.** The reviewer independently found exactly the two possible weak improvers, checked the adverse movement bound for each comparison, and checked the counterexample to the two-improver claim at radius exactly 1/3. The proof chooses a globally PO allocation by maximizing total utility **within the set of weak improvers**, not over fair allocations alone. It does not assume that fairness is generally preserved by Pareto improvements.
- **Small surplus.** The lead checked the proof in [POSITIVE.md](POSITIVE.md): with one extra good all weak improvements of a positive-utility allocation must still have exactly one pair. A new singleton cannot take either old pair good under the strict comparison (nor can the former pair holder). Thus the pair remains intact. This ceases to follow with ties or two surplus goods. Independent enumeration of all `3^4` and `3^5` assignments in the two boundary examples confirmed the improving allocations' factors `5/6` and `3/4` and zero Pareto dominators for each. Neither example disproves existence. The multiplier proof in the same note is a sufficient condition, not a characterization of all feasible instances.

**Remaining risk:** coverage is complete over the displayed parameter domains, not over arbitrary positive additive matrices. The upper bound on what is possible globally and a lower bound for these subclasses cannot be combined into a general guarantee. The prior-art search was bounded; no priority claim.

**Incidence follow-on.** The lead found the four-mixed-pair counting argument independently of the numerical optimum search. A fresh-context mathematical worker subsequently checked the construction, the per-good blocking bounds and the equality case by hand. The witness checker uses an independent route to the counts: enumerate labelled preference rows, group by symmetries preserving the valuation model, and test all fairness margins and column maxima directly. See [proof](INCIDENCE.md) and [receipt](INCIDENCE-RECEIPT.json).

**Independent one-surplus review.** A separate fresh-context reviewer checked Theorem 0, Lemma 1 and the strict-ranking theorem in POSITIVE.md, including an empty goods set, a single person, and a change of pair holder. They confirmed the proofs and identified two over-broad summaries: the closure guarantee with ties is only above a *strongly* EFX allocation, and a picking-order start must satisfy that strictness condition. Both statements were narrowed. This was a hand audit, not machine formalization or outside review; novelty remains unchecked beyond the bounded literature search.
