# Definitions and source check — 27 November 2026

[Mahabadi, Ramakrishnan and Tarnawski, *Pareto Inefficiency of EFX*, arXiv:2610.29201v2, 2 November 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.29201v2), Definitions 1–2, §4 Theorem 2, §5 Open Problem 1, Appendix A. The paper constructs the matrix; we do not claim it as a discovery. Appendix A gives a different perturbation that retains failure of full EFX under nondegeneracy. Our perturbation varies **only four entries** of §4. It is not a result about all nearby valuations.

Every good goes to exactly one agent. Utilities add, every individual value is positive, and ties count as fair. For every other bundle and every good removed from it, the observing agent must value their own bundle at least α times the remainder. Empty comparison bundles impose no test. Efficiency throughout means **integral Pareto optimality**: no complete reassignment of whole goods can weakly raise everyone's utility and strictly raise someone's. Neither fractional efficiency nor maximum total utility is required.

The paper rules out a universal factor **greater than** 2/3. It explicitly asks whether *any* absolute positive factor is compatible with PO on every positive additive instance. The existence results for approximate fairness without PO and for chores do not answer that question.

**Why the word “integral” matters.** If fractional reassignments were allowed to establish dominance, no positive universal factor could work even with two agents. In the paper's Table 1 replace the two values of the shared good by M>1, and both zeros by ε with 0<ε<1. If the shared good has a companion, the other agent gets at most one small good worth at most 1, yet sees at least M after deleting the companion. If the shared good is alone, its owner can take their preferred small good from the other agent and compensate them with ε/M of the shared good. The owner gains 1−ε and nobody loses. Thus every fractionally efficient integral allocation has factor at most 1/M; giving each small good to its high valuer and the shared good to either agent attains it and maximizes total utility. This is a counterexample to a **different** question, not to A04.
